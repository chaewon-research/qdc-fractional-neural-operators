# Research Idea Recommendation Instructions

The canonical research direction is **not** this repo's specific application. It is the user-defined theme in [the GitHub profile README](https://github.com/chaewon-research/chaewon-research/blob/main/README.md):

> How far can we reduce the computational cost of machine-learning systems without losing the internal computations or representations that are important for their capabilities?

**Before recommending an idea**: read [the mandatory research recommendation policy](https://github.com/chaewon-research/chaewon-research/blob/main/RESEARCH_RECOMMENDATION_POLICY.md) and [the retrospective audit](https://github.com/chaewon-research/chaewon-research/blob/main/RECOMMENDATION_AUDIT.md). Apply them as written. These are central instructions; do not invent local alternative versions of the user's rule.

The hard eligibility test is direct alignment with the quoted theme—not familiarity with this repository. State (C) the computational cost to remove, (I) the specific critical internal computation/representation, (P) the capability to preserve, and (T) a testable intervention measuring the tradeoff. **The four questions interpret the user's theme; they are not extra user-stated requirements.** If any part is unspecified, do not recommend the idea. A conceptually aligned *proposal* is not evidence of novelty, feasibility, or CVPR readiness.

Use current repo evidence, including negative results, and compare with relevant prior work. Consider candidates across the user's full research theme. If no idea is both eligible and credibly feasible/novel enough for the requested goal, say so.

**Local evidence caution:** QDC studies activation/weight precision sensitivity and selective numerical defect correction. Differentiate any future candidate from this existing method and respect its experiment protocols.

An AGENTS.md in this repo helps assistants which read it; it does not automatically control other sessions.
